## Recommended calculation rules

To make these definitions operational:

1\. Cutoff adherence

\\[ \frac{\text{Events meeting both cutoffs}}{\text{Eligible events}}\times100 \\]

Exclude records without an applicable cutoff from the calculation. Treat eligible records with missing booking or publication timestamps as nonadherent, while also reporting those missing timestamps separately.

2\. Missing quantities and prices

Count distinct affected records, rather than every missing field. This avoids counting one record multiple times within the same exception category.

3\. Priority exceptions

Use three severity levels:

- High: Prevents critical processing, reconciliation, or NAV reporting.
- Medium: Creates operational risk but allows processing with investigation or intervention.
- Low: Limited immediate impact; can be addressed through routine remediation.

## Keeping definitions easy to change

Rather than embedding business rules throughout the dashboard, I recommend separating them into four components\:

| Component | Purpose |
|--------|--------|
| `index.html` | Dashboard layout and presentation |
| `config.js` | Metric definitions, severity thresholds, ownership rules, cutoff rules |
| `data.js` | Simulated records, later replaceable with validated data |
| `dashboard.js` | KPI calculations, filtering, charts, and drill-down functionality |

For example, cutoff adherence would initially require both booking and publication to meet the applicable cutoff. Changing that rule later should require updating the configuration and calculation logic, not redesigning the dashboard.

The simulated dataset should include transactions, accounts, securities, positions, prices, and pipeline events, with deliberately introduced data-quality problems. This will let you demonstrate how exceptions propagate through the lifecycle.
